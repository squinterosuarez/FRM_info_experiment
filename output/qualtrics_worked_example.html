Before you start: how to read a choice. On the next screens you will compare flood protection programmes. Each one is described by the same five features. Here is one worked example to show what each row means. You do not need to choose anything on this screen.

Programme A Programme B Current System
(No Change)
Who is protected by the programme? Every household in England Households in high-risk areas Households in high-risk areas, priority for deprived areas
Who shares the cost? Everyone in England, through national taxes The local area, through local taxes Everyone in England, through national taxes
How is your household's share worked out? Households most at risk pay more Every household pays the same Wealthier households pay more
How effective is the programme? High – risk reduced to very low Low – small risk reduction Medium – risk reduced
Additional cost to your household per year £150 £75 £0

How to read each row

  • Who is protected — which households get reduced flood risk. This is about who benefits. Under Programme A everyone is protected; under Programme B only high-risk areas are.
  • Who shares the cost — whether the money is pooled across all of England (so lower-risk areas help pay for higher-risk ones) or paid by the local area (so each area funds its own protection). This is about how widely the cost is spread — under either option, your household still contributes.
  • How your household's share is worked out — the rule that sets your bill among everyone who pays: the same flat amount for all, more if you are wealthier, or more if you are at higher risk.
  • How effective — how much flood risk the programme removes.
  • Additional cost to your household — what you would pay per year, on top of what you pay now.

On the next screens, you will make real choices. There are no right or wrong answers — just pick the programme you would most prefer in each comparison.
